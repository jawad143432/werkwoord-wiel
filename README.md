# 🌷 Sterke Werkwoorden Atlas — Dutch Strong Verbs Trainer

An opinionated, offline-first companion for anyone who has ever stared at *schrijven*, *schuiven*, and *schijnen* and quietly wondered whether the Dutch language was invented to test human patience. This repository is a lovingly engineered training environment for the strong, irregular, and downright mischievous verbs of the Dutch language — the ones that refuse to follow the polite rules and instead demand to be memorized like old family recipes.

Where the original project offered a compact drill tool, the **Sterke Werkwoorden Atlas** reimagines the whole discipline as a cartography exercise. Every verb is a landmark. Every tense is a coastline. Every conjugation pattern is a river system that you slowly learn to navigate without a map — until, eventually, you *are* the map.

## 🌱 Why This Exists

Dutch strong verbs are a peculiar inheritance. They bend in the past tense, they twist in the participle, and they occasionally swap a vowel as if the whole word were auditioning for a different role. For learners, this is charming for about three minutes. After that, it becomes a recurring nightmare of *ik schrijf*, *ik schreef*, *ik heb geschreven* — with a dozen sibling patterns lurking behind each one.

Most trainers treat this as a flashcard problem. We treat it as a **navigation problem**. If you can see the terrain, you can remember the path. The Sterke Werkwoorden Atlas turns abstract conjugation tables into landscapes that your brain can actually hold onto. It is the difference between memorizing a phone book and remembering a hike through a forest you love.

This project is for:

- Students preparing for Dutch language exams who need structural clarity rather than brute force.
- Expats living in the Netherlands or Belgium who want to sound less like a textbook and more like a neighbor.
- Teachers who want a visual, respectful, and extensible reference for their classrooms.
- Polyglots who collect verb systems the way others collect stamps.
- Anyone who has ever typed *gevreest* and then felt a small, private sadness.

## 🗺️ Conceptual Overview

Imagine a world where every strong verb is a small country. Each country has three provinces: the **infinitive**, the **past tense**, and the **past participle**. Some countries share borders with others because they follow the same ablaut pattern. Others are islands — unique, proud, and slightly inconvenient.

The Atlas places all of these countries on a single, explorable canvas. You can zoom out and see the broad vowel-shift families. You can zoom in and inspect a single irregular rebel like *zijn* or *hebben*. You can trace a route from *bijten* to *gesloten* and back again.

This is not a gimmick. It is a memory palace built out of linguistics, and it happens to run beautifully on a laptop, a phone, or a tablet propped against a coffee cup on a rainy Tuesday morning in Utrecht.

## 🧬 The Verb Cartography Model

At the heart of the Atlas is a simple but powerful idea: **verbs that share a vowel-shift pattern belong together**. We group them into families, then map those families onto a two-dimensional grid where one axis represents the infinitive vowel and the other represents the past-tense vowel. The result is a kind of periodic table for Dutch strong verbs — dense, colorful, and strangely satisfying.

Each verb carries metadata:

- Infinitive form and its vowel class.
- Singular and plural past tense forms.
- Past participle, including the *ge-* prefix rules.
- Auxiliary selection (*hebben* vs *zijn*).
- Frequency tier in modern usage.
- Example sentences drawn from everyday speech.
- Optional regional notes for Flemish and Surinamese variants.

This metadata is stored in a human-readable format so that anyone with a text editor and a bit of courage can extend it. You do not need to be a programmer to contribute a verb. You need to be someone who cares about getting *gevochten* right.

## 🖥️ Screens & Experience

The application is organized around a small number of deeply considered screens.

1. **The Atlas View** — a zoomable canvas of verb landmarks, grouped by pattern family.
2. **The Drill Room** — where practice happens; timed, untimed, and endless modes.
3. **The Review Queue** — a calm, focused list of verbs due for reinforcement.
4. **The Reference Shelf** — full conjugation tables and example sentences.
5. **The Insights Dashboard** — charts, streaks, and weak-spot analysis.
6. **The Settings Alcove** — language, theme, motion, audio, and data controls.

Every screen is designed to be legible at a glance and pleasant to sit with for long sessions. We believe learning tools should respect your eyes, your time, and your attention span.

## 📱 Responsive Interface

The layout engine is fluid by design. Rather than maintaining separate mobile and desktop experiences, we use a single adaptive grid that reflows gracefully across breakpoints. On a phone, the Atlas becomes a swipeable carousel of families. On a tablet, it becomes a two-pane workspace. On a desktop, it opens into the full cartographic view with a side panel for details.

This means one codebase, one visual language, and one consistent mental model — whether you are studying on a train between Amsterdam and Rotterdam or at a desk in Groningen.

## 🌍 Multilingual Support

The interface ships with translations for English, Dutch, German, French, Spanish, and Portuguese. Additional languages can be added by dropping a new translation file into the locale directory. We deliberately keep the translation format flat and simple so that non-developers can contribute improvements without wrestling with build tooling.

Language selection is independent of the verb corpus. You can study Dutch verbs through a Spanish interface, which is a surprisingly effective way to reinforce both languages at once.

## ♿ Accessibility & Inclusive Design

Accessibility is not a feature we bolt on at the end. It is a constraint we design under from the first sketch.

- Full keyboard navigation with visible focus rings.
- Screen-reader announcements for every drill transition.
- Reduced-motion mode that respects system preferences.
- High-contrast and colorblind-safe palettes.
- Scalable typography that remains readable at 200% zoom.

We believe that a language-learning tool should be usable by as many people as possible, and that inclusivity is a form of craftsmanship.

## 🎮 Learning Modes Explained

- **Infinitive Recall** — given a past tense, produce the infinitive.
- **Past Tense Formation** — given an infinitive and a subject, conjugate correctly.
- **Participle Completion** — fill in the missing past participle.
- **Auxiliary Selection** — choose between *hebben* and *zijn* with confidence.
- **Mixed Challenge** — a shuffled gauntlet of all of the above.
- **Endless Mode** — no timer, no pressure, just verbs and quiet concentration.
- **Exam Simulation** — a timed, scored run modeled on common proficiency formats.

Each mode records its own statistics, so you can see precisely where your memory is solid and where it is still negotiating with the subjunctive of your own uncertainty.

## 🧠 Spaced Repetition Engine

The scheduling engine uses a modified Leitner-style approach with adaptive intervals. Verbs you answer correctly drift further into the future. Verbs you miss return sooner, but gently — never punitively. The goal is retention, not humiliation.

Intervals are calculated per verb, not per family, which means a single stubborn irregular will not drag the rest of its relatives into your review queue.

## 🗃️ Data Model & Verb Corpus

The corpus is stored as structured, human-readable text files. Each verb occupies its own entry with clearly labeled fields. This makes diffs readable, merges tractable, and contributions approachable.

The initial corpus covers the standard strong verb inventory used in Dutch secondary education, plus a curated set of high-frequency irregulars. Community contributions have expanded it into regional variants and rare literary forms.

## 📈 Progress, Statistics & Insights

Numbers can be motivating when they are presented with respect. The Insights Dashboard shows:

## 🧩 Localization Pipeline

Adding a new interface language involves creating a single translation file, running a validation script, and opening a pull request. We provide a checklist and a set of example entries so that contributors can work confidently even without deep technical knowledge.

Translations are reviewed for tone as well as accuracy. We care about how a language *feels*, not just whether the words technically match.

---

## 🔌 Offline-First Architecture

The Atlas is built to work without a network connection. All verb data, translations, and progress are stored locally. Optional sync, export, and import are layered on top for those who want them, but they are never required.

This is a deliberate stance. Learning should not be interrupted by a dropped connection or a service outage.

## ⚡ Performance Philosophy

We optimize for the feeling of *immediacy*. Screens respond within a frame. Drills advance without perceptible delay. Search returns results as you type. The application stays small and fast by avoiding unnecessary dependencies and by treating bundle size as a design constraint rather than an afterthought.

## 🔍 SEO & Discoverability Notes

We do not chase ranking tricks; we simply make the content excellent and let discoverability follow.
